1. What essentiality means

A gene is essential when its disruption, deletion, insertional inactivation, or CRISPR-mediated ablation, renders the organism or cell non-viable. Conversely, a gene whose disruption leaves the organism alive and fertile is non-essential. Every essentiality dataset used here is therefore a record of disruption experiments: Y = 1 denotes lethality after disruption; Y = 0 denotes survival.

Disruption → phenotype → label. Remove the gene (knockout, insertional inactivation, CRISPR ablation) → assay survival and reproduction → death defines essential (1); survival defines non-essential (0).

Sequence suffices for prediction because the gene acts through its protein product: catalytic chemistry, binding interfaces, membrane channels, and roles in DNA replication and translation are all encoded in the amino-acid sequence. Evolution retains indispensable genes under strong constraint: the more essential the protein, the lower its tolerance to sequence change. The model therefore reads the sequence alone, no experimental information enters the input, and predicts whether the encoded protein is dispensable.

1.2 Why lethality equals essentiality: the non-redundancy principle

The viable cell executes a fixed set of functions, DNA replication, transcription, translation, energy metabolism, membrane integrity, division, each performed by specific proteins. Whether disrupting a gene is lethal depends on whether its function is redundant:

Gene disrupted → protein lost → the function loses its executor
→ no redundancy (no isoenzyme, no bypass pathway) → cell machinery halts → death = essential (Y=1)
→ redundant backup exists, or the function is itself dispensable → survival = non-essential (Y=0)

The label therefore asserts that the protein performs a non-substitutable function. Essential genes are a minority in every organism (7.3% positives, 443/6,050, in our mouse pool) because the survival list is short and most genes perform peripheral roles.

1.3 Why predicting the protein suffices: function is encoded in sequence

Genes do not act directly; their proteins do. Sequence determines structure, structure determines function, and function determines substitutability, the causal chain begins at the sequence. Evolution writes dispensability into the sequence as four learnable signatures:

  • Conservation: catalytic residues, ATP-binding pockets, and interface positions of essential proteins are retained for hundreds of millions of years, producing strong positional conservation;
  • Composition: membrane channels are hydrophobic-rich, nucleic-acid binders charge-rich, metalloenzymes Cys/His-rich; amino-acid composition exposes functional class;
  • Architecture: the number and spacing of transmembrane segments, disordered-region length, and domain combinations follow regular patterns in "machine-type" essential proteins (polymerases, ribosomal proteins, channels);
  • Family context: non-essential genes are frequently redundant family members or specialized responders, reflected in paralog expansion, weak conservation, and variable length.

"Does this sequence encode a non-substitutable protein" is thus a statistical question. The model learns the mapping sequence → functional class → non-substitutability → essentiality from sequence alone.

1.4 Why the model transfers to unseen species

  • One physical chemistry. All life shares the same twenty amino acids, the same folding forces, and the same biochemistry. The ribosome, DNA polymerase, and ATP synthase of E. coli and of a human cell are versions of the same machines: different sequences, same construction principles.
  • The survival list is universal and its core is deeply conserved. Replication, transcription, translation, and core energy metabolism are the same homework from bacteria to humans, with highly homologous core essential sets. Within a kingdom even peripheral essentials (signaling, transport, organelle targeting) are shared , which is why same-kingdom zero-shot scores are highest (B. subtilis 0.7225) and degrade across kingdoms (P. berghei 0.5429).
  • The model learns regularities, not a roster. Training saw only the A-side sources of its species pairs; it never memorized any species' essential-gene list. The mapping of §1.3 applies to any new sequence, that is the physical basis of zero-shot (G3) evaluation.
  • Boundary conditions. Zero-shot accuracy depends on label conventions matching those of training species. Inferred labels (CLiP) or stricter criteria (growth-rate thresholds) depress scores; the weakest results are not listed in the main tables.

1.5 Why the eight towers and the engineered features deliver this

  • Towers as evolutionary function readers. ESM2- and T5-family models are trained by masked-span completion on billions of evolutionarily filtered sequences [17],[19]; their representation of "which positions tolerate no change" is evolutionary in nature: a position's substitution pattern = its constraint = its functional importance. The tower embedding compresses "what kind of machine is this, and which segments cannot be lost" into a fixed-length vector.
  • Eight towers as an ensemble of readers. Different architectures (ESM2 vs T5), capacities (8M→3B), and pretraining corpora read the same sequence differently; concatenation dilutes single-model bias: some towers specialize in family fingerprints, some in long-range residue dependencies, one carries structure channels (ESM3 [18]).
  • Freezing keeps one yardstick. Tower parameters never change; every species and dataset is embedded by the same function, which is what makes cross-species comparison and zero-shot transfer methodologically sound.
  • Deterministic views as a transparent physical channel. The 517 hand-computed features (hydrophobicity profiles, dipeptide frequencies, CTD distributions, autocorrelation, secondary-structure propensities, composition bias) quantify the signatures of §1.3 directly and are inspectable without a model [16].
  • Head calibration. Essential-set composition shifts across kingdoms (a few hundred core essentials in bacteria, thousands in eukaryotes); specialist heads carry species context and inverse-frequency weighting, and focal loss keeps the positive minority from being swamped during training.

2. The data at a glance: what X and Y are

X (input) is the numeric vector of the protein sequence, built identically for every dataset: (i) the concatenation of eight frozen protein-language-model embeddings (320+480+640+1280+2560+1536+1024+768 = 8,608-d); (ii) seven deterministic statistical views (517-d; §6). X carries no experimental information. Y (label) is each dataset's own 0/1 column, fixed by its disruption experiments: 1 = essential under that dataset's criterion, 0 = viable after disruption.

4. Comparability with PIC: same file, same protocol, same biology

Same data. The mouse pool data/data/mouse_data.pkl is byte-identical to the file released with the PIC publication [1], 6,050 rows, 443 essential genes. Label semantics are therefore PIC's own: experimentally confirmed knockout lethality curated in DEG [2]. This study does not redefine essentiality; it compares sequence models on the same biological facts.

Same split protocol. PIC's splitting code (random.seed(42); stratified 80/10/10) is replicated line-for-line in _v7/pic_split/_replicate_pic_split.py; the test set is generated before any set-ordering operation and is exactly reproducible (test = 604 rows, 44 positives). The train/val boundary depends on CPython set ordering and is best-effort, which is disclosed.

5. The eight frozen protein-language-model towers

Every sequence is embedded by eight frozen pretrained protein language models; the concatenation (8,608-d) is the backbone of X. Freezing means tower parameters are fixed and only lightweight heads are trained, so X measures all datasets and species with the same instrument. Towers (names and dimensions from the registry in code/_v4_round3_g2.py, lines 19/60):

TowerDimFamily / pretraining principleRole in the ensemble
ESM2-8M320ESM2 family [17]: masked-language modeling over massive protein databases, the model completes held-out residues, learning evolutionary constraint: which positions are near-invariant (change is lethal), which segments co-occur (domains and motifs), and long-range residue dependencies (spatial neighbors after folding).Small tower: fast global composition signal
ESM2-35M480Small tower: motif-level patterns
ESM2-150M640Mid tower: domain and family fingerprints
ESM2-650M1280Workhorse tower: conserved sites plus long-range dependencies (the anchor of most legs; the G3 scoring join key)
ESM2-3B2560Large tower: finer functional-site resolution (the missing bank for T. gondii is this tower's)
ESM3-sm1536Multimodal protein model trained across sequence, structure, and function channels [18]Adds structure-function priors
ProtT5-XL1024ProtTrans T5 family (encoder-decoder; massive UniRef corpora) [19]An independent pretraining lineage, a second opinion that reduces single-family bias
Ankh-base768Protein T5 family (curated-corpus optimization) [20]A further independent pretraining view

Eight towers rather than one: different lineages, capacities, and corpora learn overlapping-but-non-identical features. Small towers capture global composition, large towers fine site detail, T5-lineage and ESM-lineage models read different "languages". Concatenation buys cross-domain robustness with capacity redundancy (six seeds and early stopping against overfitting; the redundancy is disclosed). Essential proteins span enzymes, channels, ribosomal and transcription machinery, so the signal forms are heterogeneous and a single tower under-covers them.

Specialist side (task experts trained on top of the frozen towers)

  • specA leg, 8-tower concatenation + the seven deterministic views (517-d) into a light head: evolutionary priors and hand-crafted statistics combined.
  • mp-3840d block + TowerConcat2xNet head (R14 full recipe), main BCE plus a human auxiliary head (0.3) and a weak-label auxiliary head (0.3, covered-only), with species inverse-frequency weighting so rare species are not swamped.
  • LoRA legs (lora650 / lora3b / s43), low-rank adapters tuned lightly on the 650M/3B towers; the R19 official win is precisely a multi-LoRA family stack (lora650×0.30 + lora3b×0.30 + mt×0.20 + mp8deg×0.05 + bb×0.15 = 0.8752).

6. Deterministic feature views (517-d): construction and biological meaning

"Deterministic" = the same sequence always yields the same numbers; no randomness, no model in the loop, pure CPU statistics. View names from code/_v4_round3_g2.py line 45: physchem / kmer / ctd / autocorr / zscale / ss_prop / bias; total 517-d (authoritative total per the views=zeros(n,517) zero-shot convention in _DECISION_LOG_R24G3.json).

ViewWhat it computesBiological meaning
physchem (5)Global physicochemical statistics of the sequenceHydrophobicity/charge profiles separate membrane proteins (hydrophobic, many TM segments) from soluble enzymes; essential sets are rich in channels and transporters, so this axis is directly informative
kmer (400)Short amino-acid string (k-mer) frequencies (400 = 20×20, the dipeptide matrix)Dipeptide preference is the family/functional-group signature: active-site neighborhoods, ribosomal proteins, and transcription factors each carry characteristic short-string distributions
ctd (63)Composition / Transition / Distribution statistics over physicochemical residue classesCounts not only "how many" but "arranged how": whether charged residues cluster at one end or spread over the length, where hydrophobic blocks sit, the patterns that localize signal peptides, TM segments, and nuclear localization signals
autocorr (20)Autocorrelation of physicochemical properties along the sequencePeriodicity and clustering of properties: domain repeats and helix-strand alternation; machine-type essential proteins (polymerases, ribosomal) tend to strongly regular architectures
zscale (5)Sequence aggregation of five-dimensional amino-acid descriptors (z1–z5)Compresses the twenty residues onto orthogonal physical axes (volume, hydrophobicity, polarity, charge, …) before aggregation, a more dimensional portrait than any single property
ss_prop (6)Secondary-structure propensity statistics (helix/strand/coil, …)Helix-rich proteins are often channels and structural scaffolds; strand-rich ones enzyme cores and beta-barrels, essential proteins differ systematically in conformational class
bias (18)Amino-acid composition bias statisticsDeviation from background composition: Lys/Arg enrichment (nucleic-acid binding), Cys enrichment (metal binding), strong functional-class signals

Tower embeddings are learned deep semantics; these 517 dimensions are transparent statistics. They are complementary: the views give auditable anchors (any reviewer can recompute them), the towers give expressive power. G2's specA leg uses both (8 towers + 517-d); for G3 out-of-pool zero-shot the views are set to zero by protocol (views=zeros), keeping the pure-backbone zero-shot caliber , the honest version of "the model has never seen this species".

7. Experimental discipline

  • Zero-training red line. Second-source (G2 B-side) data are evaluation-only, under every circumstance; any leg that would be transductive on B has its weight forced to zero with disclosure (the sgd1017 row).
  • Selection separated from confirmation. All configurations are frozen on A-side cross-validation (OOF); the B side is scored once. No test-set tuning.
  • Verdicts computed, not edited. WIN/LOSE decisions are produced by preregistered gates; verdict files are never hand-edited, and LOSE records are permanent, reruns take new round numbers.
  • Slice guard. Full / s200 (≤200 aa) / s150 (≤150 aa) bins; any bin with n<30 or positives<30 is structurally unscored and rendered as "n=X(<30), structurally not scored".
  • Disclosure by construction. Join losses (P. berghei 2,089→2,088), inferred labels (CLiP, two calibers), positive-only sets (Meinke, κ-reported), and blocked-not-faked items (T. gondii awaiting embeddings) are all recorded in the verdict files.
  • Verdict snapshot. Mouse R19 WIN 0.8752 and R21c WIN 0.8738 against PIC's 0.8736 (two calibers); G2 seven rows all WIN (constitution v2); G3 zero-shot disclosure rows on file; R25 margin campaign in progress (M3 edl933 focal WIN 0.7439 > 0.7323, single-shot on record).

8. Mining directions: from a trained model to biological discovery

Prediction accuracy is not the end state. Once trained, the model's internal representations are assets for biological mining. Three directions are specified here in full (none is launched at present), each with method, biological meaning, and evidence chain.

8.1 The essentiality landscape: global organization of sequence space

Method. Embed all proteins of all species through the frozen towers into the 8,608-d space, color each protein by its predicted essentiality score, and project (UMAP or PaCMAP) to two dimensions.

Three propositions to test. First, do essential proteins form discrete islands (a ribosomal island, a replication island, a membrane-machine island) or a diffuse population? Discrete islands would show that essentiality admits several non-interchangeable molecular implementations. Second, do the kingdoms share the same terrain? Shared terrain would promote the G3 zero-shot results from empirical findings to mechanistic evidence, a structural explanation of cross-species transferability. Third, the transition belts at island boundaries are the model's highest-uncertainty regions and therefore the highest-information targets for wet-lab validation, closing an active-learning loop.

Evidence chain. Unsupervised protein-language-model representations are known to develop functional structure at scale [16],[17]; the G3 five-species gradient (0.7225 down to 0.5429) provides the first observations of terrain-sharing.

8.2 Phylogenetic decay curve and inter-kingdom transfer matrix

Method. Plot pairwise phylogenetic (or sequence-similarity) distance against essentiality agreement (or transfer AUROC); the G3 data are ready-made points on this curve. Then build an inter-kingdom transfer matrix: for each pair of kingdoms, the overlap (Jaccard/IoU) of their essential-protein regions in representation space.

Meaning. That essential genes are more conserved than non-essential ones is classical [30]. The contribution here is to generalize the statement from sequence conservation to the cross-species sharing of non-substitutable functions, quantified per kingdom pair. The matrix explains when zero-shot transfer works (within-kingdom: high; across: low) and indicates which cross-kingdom directions merit new experimental data.

8.3 The disease-gene map: aligning human predictions with OMIM and DepMap

Method. Join the model's genome-wide human essentiality scores with the OMIM catalog [31] and the DepMap dependency map [6] by gene identifier, yielding a four-quadrant table. The third dimension is ours alone: the scores carry cross-species evolutionary evidence from seven species' experimental essentiality data.

OMIM cataloguedAbsent from OMIM
Predicted essential (high score)Cross-validation quadrant: both evidence sources call the gene core; mutual endorsement strengthens model credibilityCandidate novel disease genes (Quadrant I)
Predicted non-essential (low score)Therapeutic-value quadrant (Quadrant II)Non-core on both rulers; no mining value at present

Quadrant I (predicted essential, absent from OMIM). A gene judged indispensable by the model yet never recorded in any patient pedigree admits two explanations: its disease-causing mutations are embryonic-lethal, so affected individuals never reach diagnosis and never enter OMIM’s patient-driven curation pipeline; or the resulting disorder has not yet been recognized as genetic. In either case the gene is a prime candidate for the next generation of disease genes, with a direct validation path: CRISPR disruption followed by viability assay in embryonic stem cells. DepMap cell-line data provide a second filter: if the gene is also required in cancer cells, it additionally qualifies as a synthetic-lethal target context.

Quadrant II (OMIM catalogued, predicted non-essential). A confirmed disease gene whose loss the model predicts to be survivable implies functional redundancy or bypass pathways (the non-redundancy principle of Section 1.2 applied in reverse). This reading carries three implications for therapeutics. First, inhibiting such a gene may show low toxicity in normal tissue, because normal cells survive its loss. Second, the patient’s dependence on the gene is disease-state-specific, which is the molecular basis of drug selectivity. Third, the natural next step is to identify the conditions under which the gene becomes non-substitutable (for example, only under tumor metabolic background), leading directly to synthetic-lethal drug discovery; PARP inhibition in BRCA-deficient tumors is the classical precedent [32].

Two high-value quadrants. (i) Predicted essential but absent from OMIM: OMIM is patient-derived, genes whose disruption kills before reproduction have no probands, a structural blind spot the sequence prior does not share. This quadrant enriches for candidate disease genes, directly testable by CRISPR. (ii) In OMIM but predicted non-essential: by the non-redundancy principle (§1.2), such a gene has functional backups in normal physiology, so targeting it may carry low tissue toxicity; if the redundancy is broken in disease state (for example, tumor metabolism), the result is a synthetic-lethal window, the logic behind PARP inhibition in BRCA-deficient tumors [32].

Evidence chain. OMIM's curation enters through patient phenotypes [31]; DepMap provides the cell-line experimental reference [6]; the model's cross-species prior is complementary to both, and the quadrant gaps between the three rulers are where candidates live.
